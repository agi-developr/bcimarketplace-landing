How to choose EEG for research

Labs should not buy on channel count alone. The real decision is cap logistics, artifact tolerance, setup burden, software fit, and whether the system matches your study design.

Best for

Universities, cognitive labs, HCI research groups, and teams running repeated participant studies.

Typical range

32–256 channels, wet or semi-dry caps, with stronger emphasis on data quality and repeatability than convenience.

Common mistake

Underestimating setup time, cap sizing, gel cleanup, and the operator training needed for clean data.

Decision criteria

What matters most

Signal quality and montage fit

Match channel count and electrode placement to your actual paradigm. More channels are not always better if setup burden crushes throughput.

Software ecosystem

Check LSL, BrainVision workflows, export formats, and compatibility with your analysis pipeline before buying.

Participant throughput

High-quality systems still fail operationally if every session becomes a 45-minute setup bottleneck.

Cap comfort and logistics

Cap sizing, consumables, cleaning, and replacement parts matter more than many teams expect.

Vendors to evaluate

Brain Products, BioSemi, ANT Neuro, g.tec, and Cognionics depending on study constraints and budget.

When to buy premium

If your publication quality, clinical rigor, or repeated high-density acquisition matters, operator efficiency alone can justify a more expensive stack.

When to avoid overbuying

For early protocol development, pilot studies, or narrow paradigms, a simpler system can get you to signal faster.
