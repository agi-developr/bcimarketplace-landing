Buying guide fNIRS

How to choose fNIRS

fNIRS can be a great fit for applied cognitive research and HCI, but buyers often underestimate montage design, motion sensitivity, and the analysis burden that comes after data capture.

Best for

Applied research, HCI studies, education experiments, and teams that care more about localization than millisecond timing.

What matters

Portability, channel layout, motion tolerance, software, and whether your experiment can absorb hemodynamic lag.

Common mistake

Choosing fNIRS without fully understanding source-detector layout and the cost of downstream analysis.

Decision criteria

What to watch for

Motion and real-world use

Portable systems are attractive, but motion artifacts can destroy value if the study design is too dynamic.

Analysis pipeline

The purchase is not just hardware. You are also buying a preprocessing and interpretation workflow.

Coverage strategy

Ask whether you need broad cortical coverage or a narrower, better-targeted montage.

Operational burden

Check training, cap fit, consumables, and whether your team can actually run the system consistently.

Vendors to evaluate

Kernel, NIRx, Artinis, and Gowerlabs are common starting points depending on budget and study design.

When fNIRS wins

When your use case can tolerate slower hemodynamic response but benefits from better localization than lightweight EEG options.

When EEG wins instead

When timing, portability, or software ecosystem simplicity matter more than imaging-adjacent signal characteristics.
